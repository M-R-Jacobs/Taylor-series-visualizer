# Taylor-series-visualizer
Taylor series (developed by the English mathematician Brook Taylor, 1715) approximate the behavior of any function $f(x)$ as an n-th degree polynomial function, but namely help with reducing transcendental functions like: 
$$f(x)=sin x$$
$$f(x)=e^x$$
$$f(x)=ln x$$
$$f(x)=x^x$$
This is often useful in simplifying complex results or solving differential equations in physics and math to expressions/equations that can condense/reduce in ways that would have been more difficult had you kept the transcendental form. For the purposes of gaining physical, real world intuition (where perfect accuracy is not necessary), Taylor approximations are critical. Applications further highlight the impact of Taylor series: making calculations in your calculator possible or easier, helping GPS recievers calculate your exact position, improving the gradient-based optimization procedures in AI LLM's; Euler's famous formula (which will be immediately recognized by physicists and engineers) also is given by this method.
$$e^{i\pi}=-1 \quad \text{or} \quad e^{2i\pi}=1$$
